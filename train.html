Chapter 12 · Hands-on

Train your first policy

One command turns your 50 demonstrations into an ACT policy. Your job is to choose where it runs, start it, and read what it tells you.

10 minutes of your time, 30 minutes to several hours of the computer'sYou need: your dataset on the Hub

What happens during training

Everything from chapter 9, at scale. The program:

  1. Downloads your dataset and creates an ACT network with about 80 million random parameters.
  2. Picks a batch of 8 random moments from your episodes. For each: the camera pictures and joint values at that moment, and the chunk of actions you performed next.
  3. Asks the network to predict those action chunks, and measures the gap between prediction and what you really did. That gap is the loss.
  4. Nudges all 80 million parameters to shrink the loss slightly.
  5. Repeats steps 2–4 tens of thousands of times, saving a checkpoint every so often.

The robot is not involved and can be switched off. Training only needs the data.

Choose where to train

In chapter 4 you checked for a GPU. That decides your route. The time estimates are from LeRobot's hardware guide for about 50 episodes, and the guide itself says they can be off by half in either direction.

Your computerRouteRough time for ACT
NVIDIA GPU with 6 GB memory or moreA: train locally30–60 min on a top card (RTX 4090/3090); a few hours on a laptop card
Apple Silicon MacA: train locally with mps, or B for speed6–14 hours on a high-end Mac; leave it overnight
No GPUB: train in the cloud1–2 hours on a rented mid-range GPU
Do not train on a plain processor

The official guidance for a computer without a GPU is two words: "Don't train." It would take days. Use route B.

  1. Run the training command

    Activate the environment, then run this with your username. Change cuda to mps on a Mac.

    lerobot-train --dataset.repo_id=YOUR_NAME/so101_pick_place --policy.type=act --output_dir=outputs/train/act_so101_pick_place --job_name=act_so101_pick_place --policy.device=cuda --policy.repo_id=YOUR_NAME/act_so101_pick_place --steps=30000 --trackio.enable=true
    lerobot-train \
  --dataset.repo_id=YOUR_NAME/so101_pick_place \
  --policy.type=act \
  --output_dir=outputs/train/act_so101_pick_place \
  --job_name=act_so101_pick_place \
  --policy.device=cuda \
  --policy.repo_id=YOUR_NAME/act_so101_pick_place \
  --steps=30000 \
  --trackio.enable=true
    lerobot-train --dataset.repo_id=… --policy.type=act --output_dir=… --job_name=… --policy.device=cuda --policy.repo_id=… --steps=30000 --trackio.enable=true

  2. Confirm it is using the GPU

    In the first lines of output, look for a warning like this:

    WARNING: Device 'cuda' is not available. Switching to 'cpu'.

    If you see it, stop with Ctrl+C. Your PyTorch has no GPU support; go back to step 4 of chapter 4.

  3. Let it run

    Keep the computer awake and plugged in. If training is interrupted, you can continue from the last checkpoint:

    lerobot-train --config_path=outputs/train/act_so101_pick_place/checkpoints/last/pretrained_model/train_config.json --resume=true

Your dataset is already on the Hub, so any computer on the internet can train on it. Two options:

Option 1: Google Colab

Colab gives you a temporary computer with a GPU inside a web page. Hugging Face publishes a ready-made notebook for ACT.

  1. Open the official ACT training notebook and sign in with a Google account.
  2. In the menu choose Runtime → Change runtime type and select a GPU.
  3. Run the cells from the top with the play buttons. They install LeRobot and ask you to log in to Hugging Face with your token.
  4. In the training cell, replace the example dataset and policy names with yours (YOUR_NAME/so101_pick_place and YOUR_NAME/act_so101_pick_place).
  5. Keep the browser tab open until it finishes. The policy is uploaded to your Hub account at the end.
Two things to know

When this course was written, the notebook's training cell still contained the option --wandb.enable=False, which belongs to an older LeRobot version. If the cell fails complaining about it, delete that line and run the cell again.

The free tier offers a modest GPU and may disconnect long sessions. The notebook's quoted times assume a much faster paid GPU, and we could not find a verified time for the free one. If a session dies, lower --steps to 20000 and try again.

Option 2: Hugging Face Jobs

LeRobot can send the training to Hugging Face's own computers by adding one option. You run this from your own terminal, and it needs a payment method on your Hugging Face account because GPU time is billed by the second. Ask your teacher before using it.

lerobot-train --dataset.repo_id=YOUR_NAME/so101_pick_place --policy.type=act --policy.repo_id=YOUR_NAME/act_so101_pick_place --steps=30000 --job.target=a10g-small

--job.target names the rented machine. hf jobs hardware lists the choices and prices; hf jobs logs <job-id> shows progress.

Reading the training log

Every 200 steps a line like this appears:

step:2K smpl:16K ep:18 epch:0.36 loss:0.412 grdn:21.3 lr:1.0e-05 updt_s:0.19 data_s:0.01
step:2K smpl:16K ep:18 epch:0.36 loss:0.412 grdn:21.3 lr:1.0e-05 updt_s:0.19 data_s:0.01

The numbers in this example are illustrative. Yours will differ.

What you seeMeaningWhat to do
Loss falls fast, then flattensNormal, healthy trainingNothing
Loss barely moves from the startThe model is not learningCheck the dataset name and that episodes actually contain movement
Loss is nanThe maths blew upStop; check the dataset in the viewer for corrupted episodes
CUDA out of memoryThe batch does not fit in your GPU's memoryAdd --batch_size=4 (and double --steps to see the same amount of data)
The loss cannot tell you if the robot will succeed

This is chapter 9's warning in practice. The loss measures how well the network reproduces your recorded actions on your recorded images. A low loss means it fits the demonstrations. It cannot reveal that the camera has since moved, that the demos never covered the left side of the table, or that small errors will snowball. Only running the robot answers that. Treat the loss as "training did not break", not as a grade.

Where the result is

  • On the training computer: outputs/train/act_so101_pick_place/checkpoints/ holds numbered folders (for example 020000) and last. Inside each, the pretrained_model folder is the policy.
  • On the Hub: at huggingface.co/YOUR_NAME/act_so101_pick_place, uploaded automatically when training finishes.

A checkpoint is nothing more than a file with 80 million numbers plus a description of the network's shape. Those numbers are your policy.
