Chapter 8 · Hands-on

Teleoperate and add cameras

The moment it comes alive: you move the leader and the follower copies you. Then you give the robot eyes.

30–45 minutesYou need: both arms calibrated, one or two USB cameras

Part A: first movement

  1. Get ready

    Both arms clamped, powered and plugged in by USB. Clear the table around the follower. Hold the leader in roughly the same pose as the follower before you start, so the follower does not jump.

  2. Run teleoperation

    lerobot-teleoperate --robot.type=so101_follower --robot.port=COM5 --robot.id=my_follower_arm --teleop.type=so101_leader --teleop.port=COM6 --teleop.id=my_leader_arm
    lerobot-teleoperate \
    --robot.type=so101_follower \
    --robot.port=/dev/ttyACM0 \
    --robot.id=my_follower_arm \
    --teleop.type=so101_leader \
    --teleop.port=/dev/ttyACM1 \
    --teleop.id=my_leader_arm
    lerobot-teleoperate --robot.type … --robot.port … --robot.id … --teleop.type … --teleop.port … --teleop.id …

  3. Move the leader

    Move slowly at first. The follower should mirror every joint, including the gripper when you squeeze the trigger. Stop with Ctrl+C in the terminal.

    When you stop, the arm goes limp

    Stopping the program releases the motors and the follower sinks under its own weight. Lower it onto the table before pressing Ctrl+C, or hold it.

What just happened

About 30 times per second the program read six calibrated joint values from the leader and sent them as goals to the follower. The follower's servos did the rest with their internal feedback loops. You are, right now, the robot's policy: your eyes observe, your brain decides, your hand outputs six numbers. Training will replace you in this loop and keep everything else the same.

If it does not behave

SymptomLikely causeFix
Follower does the opposite, or one joint is offsetCalibration of one arm was offRecalibrate both arms (chapter 7), taking care with the middle pose
The wrong arm moves, or nothing movesThe two ports are swappedSwap the port values in the command, or rerun lerobot-find-port
One leader joint feels wrong and its follower joint moves oddly fast or slowA leader motor with the wrong gearing is in that jointCheck the code on that motor against the table in chapter 6
Follower shakes while holding stillLoose screws or a joint under strainPower off, tighten horn screws, check nothing is rubbing

Part B: cameras

So far the robot has only proprioception: it knows its own joint angles. It has no idea where the object is. Cameras fix that.

Where to put them

The official tutorial uses two cameras: one looking down or forward at the workspace (named top or front), and one mounted on the follower's wrist (named wrist). One fixed camera is enough to begin.

The rule that decides everything

The docs give one test: "could you do the task yourself by only looking at the camera images?" The policy has no other information about the world. If the object is hidden behind the gripper in the picture, or too dark to see, the policy is blind to it no matter how well it is trained.

  • Fix cameras rigidly. Tape or clamp them. A camera that moves between recording and running the policy shows the policy a world it has never seen.
  • Light evenly. Use steady, diffuse light. Avoid direct sunlight and moving shadows; the docs note that "lighting matters more than resolution".
  • Keep the leader arm and people out of the picture. When the policy runs, the leader will not be there. Anything in the demonstrations that will be missing later can confuse it.
  • Modest resolution. 640×480 at 30 frames per second is the recommended setting. Bigger images slow down recording and training without helping most tasks.

Find your cameras

  1. Plug in the cameras and run:

    lerobot-find-cameras opencv
    --- Detected Cameras ---
Camera #0:
  Name: OpenCV Camera @ 0
  Type: OpenCV
  Id: 0
  …
Camera #1:
  Name: OpenCV Camera @ 1
  Id: 1

    Each camera gets an index (0, 1, 2…). The program also saves one test photo from each camera into the folder outputs/captured_images. Open those photos to learn which index is which camera. A laptop's built-in webcam is usually one of them.

    Indexes can change

    Unplugging a camera or restarting can reshuffle the numbers. If the pictures look swapped one day, run this command again.

  2. Teleoperate with cameras and a live view

    Add two options to the teleoperate command. Replace the index numbers with yours.

    lerobot-teleoperate --robot.type=so101_follower --robot.port=COM5 --robot.id=my_follower_arm --robot.cameras="{ top: {type: opencv, index_or_path: 1, width: 640, height: 480, fps: 30}, wrist: {type: opencv, index_or_path: 0, width: 640, height: 480, fps: 30} }" --teleop.type=so101_leader --teleop.port=COM6 --teleop.id=my_leader_arm --display_data=true
    lerobot-teleoperate \
    --robot.type=so101_follower \
    --robot.port=/dev/ttyACM0 \
    --robot.id=my_follower_arm \
    --robot.cameras="{ top: {type: opencv, index_or_path: 1, width: 640, height: 480, fps: 30}, wrist: {type: opencv, index_or_path: 0, width: 640, height: 480, fps: 30} }" \
    --teleop.type=so101_leader \
    --teleop.port=/dev/ttyACM1 \
    --teleop.id=my_leader_arm \
    --display_data=true
    --robot.cameras="{ top: {type: opencv, index_or_path: 1, width: 640, height: 480, fps: 30}, wrist: {…} }" --display_data=true

    With only one camera, use --robot.cameras="{ front: {type: opencv, index_or_path: 0, width: 640, height: 480, fps: 30} }".

  3. Do the camera test

    A viewer window opens. Put an object on the table and try to pick it up while looking only at the viewer window, not at the real robot. If that is hard, move the camera or improve the light until it is easy. This ten-minute test saves hours of wasted recording.

This camera setup is now frozen

The docs are explicit: "A policy trained on this dataset expects the same camera names and resolution when you deploy it". Copy your exact --robot.cameras="…" text into a notes file. You will paste the identical text when recording and when running the policy.

No spare webcam? Use a phone

On a Mac, an iPhone signed in with the same Apple ID appears as a camera automatically (Continuity Camera; macOS 13+ and iOS 16+).

On any other combination, the docs suggest the DroidCam app on the phone together with OBS Studio and its virtual camera on the computer, with the resolution set to 640×480 in both. It works but takes patience; a cheap USB webcam is simpler.

Camera troubleshooting

SymptomFix
Wrong picture under a camera nameIndexes changed. Rerun lerobot-find-cameras opencv and update the numbers
Error that the frame width or height does not matchThe camera cannot deliver 640×480. Use the width and height that lerobot-find-cameras reported for it
macOS: black picture or no camera foundSystem Settings → Privacy & Security → Camera: allow your Terminal app, then restart it
Two cameras work alone but not togetherPlug them into different USB sockets on the computer, not the same hub. Two identical camera models can also confuse the system; mixing brands avoids it
Movement feels laggy with cameras onLower the resolution, or close other programs using the cameras
