A step-by-step course for the LeRobot SO-ARM101 kit

Build a robot arm and teach it a task

You start with a box of parts and no experience. You finish with a robot that picks up an object by itself, and you will understand why it works, why it sometimes fails, and what the words policy, ACT, VLA and world model really mean.

This is the heart of the kitsimulation

You hold the leader arm; the follower copies you. Record yourself doing a task this way about fifty times, and a neural network can learn to move the follower without you. Move the sliders to try it.

The chapters

    What you need

    • An SO-101 kit: two arms (leader and follower) with motors, control boards, power supplies and cables. Chapter 3 helps you identify your version.
    • A computer running Windows, macOS (Apple Silicon) or Linux, with about 10 GB of free space.
    • At least one USB camera. A second one helps.
    • A small screwdriver set, a table edge to clamp to, and a small object and box for the task.
    • A graphics card is not required. Without one, the training step runs in the cloud.
    • Time: roughly 10–15 hours in total, spread over several sessions.
    Before you plug anything in

    Read chapter 3 first. Some kits contain a 12V and a 5V power supply, and connecting the 12V one to the wrong arm can destroy its motors.

    Written for LeRobot 0.7.0 (November 2026). All commands come from the official documentation; see sources for references and for what could not be verified.
