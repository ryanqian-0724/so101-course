Chapter 6 · Hands-on

Assemble the arms

You build each arm from the table upward: base, shoulder, elbow, wrist, hand. The two arms are built the same way until the very last step.

About 2 hours for the follower, 1.5 for the leaderYou need: motors with IDs set, printed parts, screws, screwdriver

Times are from people who have built the kit; the official guide gives none. Working in pairs is faster and catches mistakes.

Before you start

Watch, then do

Text can tell you which screws; only video shows how the parts sit. For each joint: watch the short official video once, then build with this page as your checklist. The steps here follow the official LeRobot SO-101 guide.

The three things you will handle

Motor horn

A round disc that pushes onto the motor's shaft. The arm's plastic parts screw onto the horn, so when the shaft turns, the part turns. Most motors get two: one on the shaft (top) and a free-spinning one on the opposite side (bottom) so the joint is held from both sides.

M2×6 screws

The smallest screws. They hold a motor's body into its plastic holder. "M2" means 2 mm thread diameter, "6" means 6 mm long.

M3×6 screws

The thicker screws. They fix a horn to its shaft and fix plastic parts to the horns.

Three habits that prevent rebuilds
  • Read the label before every motor goes in. Joint 3 of the leader takes L3, nothing else.
  • Plug a 3-wire cable into each motor as you place it, and route cables as you go. Builders report that threading cables through a finished arm is the most frustrating part.
  • Snug, not tight. You are screwing metal into plastic. Stop turning when the screw stops; forcing it strips the plastic thread.

Build the follower

Start with the follower (motors F1–F6).

  1. Joint 1: base and shoulder pan (motor 1)

    Watch the Joint 1 video

    • Push both horns onto motor 1. Fix the top horn with one M3×6 screw. The bottom horn needs no screw.
    • Place the motor into the base.
    • Fasten it with four M2×6 screws: two from the top, two from the bottom.
    • Slide the first motor holder over it and fasten with two M2×6 screws, one each side.
    • Attach the shoulder part with four M3×6 screws on top and four on the bottom.
    • Add the shoulder motor holder.

  2. Joint 2: shoulder lift (motor 2)

    Watch the Joint 2 video

    • Fit both horns; M3×6 screw in the top horn only.
    • Slide motor 2 in from the top and fasten with four M2×6 screws.
    • Attach the upper arm with four M3×6 screws on each side.

  3. Joint 3: elbow (motor 3)

    Watch the Joint 3 video

    • Fit both horns; M3×6 screw in the top horn only.
    • Insert motor 3 and fasten with four M2×6 screws.
    • Connect the forearm to motor 3 with four M3×6 screws on each side.

  4. Joint 4: wrist flex (motor 4)

    Watch the Joint 4 video

    • Fit both horns; M3×6 screw in the top horn only.
    • Slide motor holder 4 over, then slide in motor 4.
    • Fasten with four M2×6 screws.

  5. Joint 5: wrist roll (motor 5)

    Watch the Joint 5 video

    • Insert motor 5 into the wrist holder and secure with two M2×6 screws from the front.
    • Fit only one horn on this motor and secure it with an M3×6 screw.
    • Secure the wrist to motor 4 with four M3×6 screws on both sides.

  6. Gripper (motor 6): follower only

    Watch the Gripper video

    • Attach the gripper body to the horn on motor 5 with four M3×6 screws.
    • Insert the gripper motor and secure with two M2×6 screws on each side.
    • Fit both horns on it; M3×6 screw in the top horn only.
    • Install the moving claw with four M3×6 screws on both sides.

  7. Wire it up

    Connect the motors in a chain with the 3-wire cables: motor 6 → 5 → 4 → 3 → 2 → 1, and finally motor 1 to the control board. Each motor has two identical sockets, so one cable comes in and one goes out. Attach the control board to the base of the arm.

    Before closing anything up, move every joint by hand through its whole range and watch the cables. None should be pulled tight, sharply bent or caught in a joint.

Why a chain and not six separate cables?

Six cables running from the base to each motor would form a thick bundle that fights every movement. With a chain, each segment of the arm carries one short cable to its neighbour. It works because the motors share the wires as a bus and are addressed by ID, which is exactly why you set the IDs first.

Then, in place of the gripper, build the handle:

  1. Handle and trigger (motor 6): leader only

    Watch the Leader handle video

    • Mount the leader holder onto the wrist with four M3×6 screws.
    • Attach the handle to the leader holder with one M2×6 screw.
    • Insert the gripper motor, secure with two M2×6 screws on each side, and fix one horn with an M3×6 screw.
    • Attach the trigger with four M3×6 screws.

    Wire the leader in the same chain, 6 → 1 → board.

Clamp both arms to the table

Use the table clamps to fix each base to the table edge, with the two arms side by side and far enough apart that they cannot touch each other at full stretch. Put the leader on the side of your writing hand.

Why clamp?

When the arm stretches forward, its weight is far from the base, and the base wants to tip, like holding a heavy bag at arm's length. The clamp also keeps the base in exactly the same place from day to day, which matters later: a policy learns where things are relative to the robot's base and the cameras. Move the base and yesterday's training no longer matches today's world.
