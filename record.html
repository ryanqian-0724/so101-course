Chapter 11 · Hands-on

Record a dataset

This is the most important hands-on chapter. The policy can only ever be as good as the demonstrations you give it. Researchers have a blunt saying for it: garbage in, garbage out.

1.5–2 hoursYou need: working teleoperation with cameras, an object and a container

What exactly gets recorded

An episode is one complete demonstration, from start pose to finished task. While you teleoperate, LeRobot saves one row of data 30 times per second. Explore a (simulated) episode:

Try it: scrub through one episode5 seconds = 150 frames

Drag the frame slider and watch the numbers. action is where the leader is: the command. observation.state is where the follower actually is. Notice the follower trails slightly behind, and the numbers differ most when the arm moves fast. The policy will learn: "when the picture and state look like this, the command was that".

Why is the action the leader's position, not the follower's?

Because the policy's job is to produce commands. The follower's measured position is the result of a command a moment earlier, after lag and gravity. If the policy learned to output "where the follower was", it would be commanding the arm to stay where it already is, and the arm would barely move.

On disk, a LeRobot dataset (format version 3.0) is a folder:

PartContainsFile type
data/The rows of numbers: joint states, actions, timestampsParquet (a compact table format)
videos/The camera footage, one video stream per cameraMP4
meta/Descriptions: frame rate, task sentence, episode lengths, and statistics such as the average and range of every jointJSON and Parquet

Design your task before recording

The official starting task is: pick up an object from different places on the table and put it in a container. Keep it that simple for your first dataset.

The plan: 50 episodes, 10 at each of 5 positions

That is the official suggestion: "at least 50 episodes, with 10 episodes per location". The team behind it reported that the same setup with only 25 episodes "was not enough".

Keep the same in every episodeVary between episodes
Camera positions and lightingObject position (your 5 marks)
How you grasp (same side, same approach)Object rotation, slightly
Where the container isSmall differences in the arm's starting pose
Start and end with the arm in a rest pose

The reasons are in chapter 10: variety in the situation forces the policy to use its camera and widens what it can handle; consistency in your strategy avoids the averaging trap. The docs add a warning: "Avoid adding too much variation too quickly, as it may hinder your results."

Record

  1. Build your command

    It is the teleoperate command from chapter 8 with a new program name and a few --dataset options. Use your ports, your exact camera text, and your Hugging Face username in place of YOUR_NAME.

    lerobot-record --robot.type=so101_follower --robot.port=COM5 --robot.id=my_follower_arm --robot.cameras="{ top: {type: opencv, index_or_path: 1, width: 640, height: 480, fps: 30}, wrist: {type: opencv, index_or_path: 0, width: 640, height: 480, fps: 30} }" --teleop.type=so101_leader --teleop.port=COM6 --teleop.id=my_leader_arm --dataset.repo_id=YOUR_NAME/so101_pick_place --dataset.no_stamp=true --dataset.num_episodes=50 --dataset.single_task="Pick up the red brick and put it in the box" --dataset.episode_time_s=30 --dataset.reset_time_s=10 --dataset.streaming_encoding=true --display_data=true
    lerobot-record \
    --robot.type=so101_follower \
    --robot.port=/dev/ttyACM0 \
    --robot.id=my_follower_arm \
    --robot.cameras="{ top: {type: opencv, index_or_path: 1, width: 640, height: 480, fps: 30}, wrist: {type: opencv, index_or_path: 0, width: 640, height: 480, fps: 30} }" \
    --teleop.type=so101_leader \
    --teleop.port=/dev/ttyACM1 \
    --teleop.id=my_leader_arm \
    --dataset.repo_id=YOUR_NAME/so101_pick_place \
    --dataset.no_stamp=true \
    --dataset.num_episodes=50 \
    --dataset.single_task="Pick up the red brick and put it in the box" \
    --dataset.episode_time_s=30 \
    --dataset.reset_time_s=10 \
    --dataset.streaming_encoding=true \
    --display_data=true
    lerobot-record --robot… --teleop… --dataset.repo_id=YOUR_NAME/so101_pick_place --dataset.no_stamp=true --dataset.num_episodes=50 --dataset.single_task="…" --dataset.episode_time_s=30 --dataset.reset_time_s=10 --dataset.streaming_encoding=true --display_data=true

  2. Learn the three keys

    KeyEffectUse it when
    → or nEnd this episode (or this reset pause) now and move onYou finished the task before the time ran out
    ← or rThrow away the current episode and record it againYou fumbled, dropped the object, or bumped the camera
    Esc or qStop the whole session, save, and uploadYou need to stop early

    If the keys do nothing, click on the terminal window so it has keyboard focus.

  3. Do a 3-episode rehearsal first

    Run the command once with --dataset.num_episodes=3 and the name YOUR_NAME/so101_test. Get used to the rhythm: the terminal tells you an episode has started, you perform the task, press →, reset the object, repeat. Then check the result in step 5 before investing an hour.

  4. Record the real 50

    Run the full command. Work through your five positions, ten episodes each. Good habits:

    • Start each episode with the arm in the rest pose and end with the gripper open and away from the box.
    • Move at a calm, steady speed. Do not rush and do not freeze.
    • If you make a real mess, press ←. A small wobble that you correct is fine to keep.
    • Keep hands, sleeves and the leader arm out of the camera view.
    • Take a break halfway. Tired demonstrations are sloppy demonstrations.

    When the last episode ends, LeRobot finishes the videos and uploads the dataset to the Hub. Wait for the prompt to return.

  5. Check the timing report

    After each episode the terminal prints a line like this:

    Cadence (episode 0): 29.88 Hz vs 30 Hz target …

    The first number must be close to 30. If it is well below (say 22), your computer could not keep up, usually because of the cameras.

    Why this matters

    The dataset does not store real clock times. It assumes frames are exactly 1/30 of a second apart. If the real rate was 22 per second, the saved episode claims your movements were faster than they were, and the policy learns a sped-up, wrong version of the motion. Fix it before recording more: lower the camera resolution, close other programs, or use one camera.

  6. Look at your data

    Open the online viewer at huggingface.co/spaces/lerobot/visualize_dataset, type your dataset name (YOUR_NAME/so101_pick_place) and browse the episodes. Sign in there if your dataset is private. You will see all cameras playing together with graphs of every joint.

    Watch at least ten episodes and ask:

    • Is the object visible the whole time in at least one camera?
    • Is the picture sharp and evenly lit?
    • Does every episode show the complete task?
    • Are any episodes clearly bad?

    You can also replay an episode on the real robot. The follower repeats your recorded commands with no leader attached:

    lerobot-replay --robot.type=so101_follower --robot.port=COM5 --robot.id=my_follower_arm --dataset.repo_id=YOUR_NAME/so101_pick_place --dataset.episode=0
    lerobot-replay \
    --robot.type=so101_follower \
    --robot.port=/dev/ttyACM0 \
    --robot.id=my_follower_arm \
    --dataset.repo_id=YOUR_NAME/so101_pick_place \
    --dataset.episode=0
    A revealing experiment

    Put the object where it was in episode 0 and replay episode 0: the robot picks it up. Now move the object 5 cm and replay again: the robot grabs at empty air. Replay is "blind" playback. This is precisely the difference between a recording and a policy: a policy looks at the camera and adapts.

Fixing a dataset afterwards

Delete bad episodes or add more

Delete episodes (for example numbers 3 and 17) into a new, cleaned copy:

lerobot-edit-dataset --repo_id YOUR_NAME/so101_pick_place --new_repo_id YOUR_NAME/so101_pick_place_clean --operation.type delete_episodes --operation.episode_indices "[3, 17]"

Add more episodes to an existing dataset: run your record command again with two extra options, --resume=true and --dataset.root= followed by the dataset's folder on your computer. Set --dataset.num_episodes to the number of additional episodes, not the new total.

Where is it on my computer? In the hidden folder .cache/huggingface/lerobot/YOUR_NAME/ inside your user folder.

ProblemFix
