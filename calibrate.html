Chapter 7 · Hands-on

Calibrate

The arms are built, but the computer does not yet know what "straight" or "fully bent" means for your particular arms. Calibration teaches it.

15 minutesYou need: both arms clamped, powered and connected by USB

The problem calibration solves

Each servo's encoder reports its angle as a raw number from 0 to 4095. But where "0" lies depends on how the horn happened to be pushed onto the shaft during assembly. Two arms built by two people, placed in exactly the same pose, will report different raw numbers.

That is a disaster for teleoperation: the leader reads "2300" at its elbow, sends "2300" to the follower, and the follower's elbow, whose numbers are shifted, goes somewhere else.

Try it: one command, two armssimulation

Both arms receive the same command. Uncalibrated, each lands in a different wrong pose, because each motor's "middle" is shifted by a different amount. Switch to "Calibrated": the software now subtracts each motor's own shift, and both arms match the dashed target.

Calibration records two things for every motor and saves them in a small file:

  • The middle: the raw number the motor reports when the joint is in the centre of its travel.
  • The range: the smallest and largest raw numbers the joint can physically reach.

From then on, LeRobot translates raw numbers into a shared, meaningful scale. "Elbow at 40" means the same physical bend on the leader, the follower, and anybody else's SO-101.

Why it matters beyond teleoperation

The official docs put it this way: calibration "allows a neural network trained on one robot to work on another". A policy only ever sees numbers. If the numbers mean different poses on different days or different arms, nothing it learned is valid. Calibration is what makes the numbers trustworthy.

Analogy

Two kitchen scales that both show "37 g" when empty will disagree with every recipe. Pressing the "zero" button on each fixes it. Calibration is pressing "zero" on twelve motors, and also telling each how far it can go.

Calibrate the follower

  1. Power and connect

    Plug in the follower's power supply and USB cable. If you are unsure of the port, run lerobot-find-port again (chapter 5).

  2. Start calibration

    lerobot-calibrate --robot.type=so101_follower --robot.port=COM5 --robot.id=my_follower_arm
    lerobot-calibrate \
    --robot.type=so101_follower \
    --robot.port=/dev/ttyACM0 \
    --robot.id=my_follower_arm

    On a Mac the port looks like /dev/tty.usbmodem575E0032081.

    lerobot-calibrate --robot.type=so101_follower --robot.port=… --robot.id=my_follower_arm

  3. Hold the arm in the middle pose, press Enter

    The program asks you to move every joint to the middle of its range. Hold the arm so that:

    • it points straight forward (pan centred),
    • the upper arm, forearm and hand are each roughly halfway between their two extremes,
    • the gripper is half open.

    The program prints a sketch of the pose, and the official calibration video shows it. It does not need to be exact. Press Enter while holding it.

  4. Move every joint through its full range

    A live table appears with a row per joint and columns MIN, POS, MAX. Now, one joint at a time, move it slowly all the way to one end stop and all the way to the other. Watch MIN and MAX spread apart as you go.

    NAME            |    MIN |    POS |    MAX
shoulder_pan    |    758 |   2046 |   3301
shoulder_lift   |    812 |   2051 |   3190
elbow_flex      |    901 |   2040 |   3077
…

    Your numbers will differ. wrist_roll can turn all the way round, so the program handles it automatically; do not worry if its row does not react.

    When every joint has been to both ends, press Enter.

  5. Read where the file was saved

    Calibration saved to …/calibration/robots/…/my_follower_arm.json

    That file is the result. It lives in a hidden folder in your user directory, and the same values are also written into the motors.

    Now unplug the arm's power for five seconds and plug it back in. Builders have reported that, without this restart, a joint can occasionally swing hard to its end stop the first time the motors switch on after calibration.

Calibrate the leader

Same procedure with the leader. Because it is a teleoperator, the option names start with --teleop:

lerobot-calibrate --teleop.type=so101_leader --teleop.port=COM6 --teleop.id=my_leader_arm
lerobot-calibrate \
    --teleop.type=so101_leader \
    --teleop.port=/dev/ttyACM1 \
    --teleop.id=my_leader_arm
Write these four things down

You will type them in every command for the rest of the course: the follower's port, the leader's port, my_follower_arm and my_leader_arm. Ports can change after a restart; the ids must never change.

When to recalibrate

Only when the hardware changes: you replaced a motor, took a horn off and put it back, or rebuilt a joint. Otherwise the saved file is reused automatically.
