Chapter 3 · Hands-on

What is in your kit

SO-101 kits are sold by many shops in different versions. Spend fifteen minutes working out exactly which one you have. One mistake here, the wrong power supply on the wrong arm, can burn out motors.

15 minutesYou need: the unopened kit, a marker pen

Step 1: which kind of kit is it?

Open the box and answer three questions.

You have a pre-assembled kit. Both arms are in one piece. In Seeed Studio's assembled kit the leader is black and the follower is white; other shops use other colours.

You can skip chapter 5 and chapter 6 as tasks, but please still read them: you will need to understand motor IDs and how the arm is put together when something comes loose. After setting up your computer, go to chapter 7.

You have an unassembled kit with printed parts. You should find 12 servo motors, two bags of plastic parts (the 3D-printed pieces), two small circuit boards, cables, screws and clamps. This is the most common kit and this course covers every step.

You have a motors-only kit. The plastic structure must be 3D-printed before you can assemble. See 3D printing the parts at the bottom of this page. You can do chapters 4 and 5 (computer setup and motor IDs) while the parts print.

Step 2: find the two power supplies and read their labels

This is the most important check on the page. Every power supply has small print on it saying OUTPUT followed by a voltage, for example 5V ⎓ 4A or 12V ⎓ 2A.

What you findYour editionWhich supply goes where
Two supplies, both 5VStandardEither one on either arm
One 5V and one 12VProLeader: 5V only. Follower: 12V.
Anything else (for example two 12V)Another vendor's variantFollow that vendor's instructions for each arm, and check the voltage printed on the motors
Danger: do not mix up the power supplies

The leader arm's motors are always the 7.4-volt type, which run on a 5V supply. Plugging a 12V supply into them can destroy them. The vendor's warning is blunt: "watch out that you plug in the right power supply if you have 12V and 7.4V motors, otherwise you might burn your motors!"

Do this now: if you have a 12V supply, put a piece of tape on its plug and write FOLLOWER ONLY on it.

Why are there two voltages?

The same servo is made in a 7.4V version and a 12V version. Higher voltage pushes more current through the motor, so it is stronger: about 16.5 kg·cm of turning force for the 7.4V type against 30 kg·cm for the 12V type. The follower does the lifting, so "Pro" kits give it the stronger motors. The leader only needs to be read as a sensor, so it always gets the cheaper 7.4V ones. A motor built for 7.4V cannot survive 12V, just as a 5V phone charger port would not survive mains electricity.

"kg·cm" means: at 1 cm from the shaft the motor can hold that many kilograms. At 10 cm, a tenth of that.

Step 3: sort the motors

An unassembled kit has 12 servos that look almost identical. They are not. Each has a sticker with a model code, and the code tells you the gear ratio.

Code on the motorVoltageGear ratioHow manyWhere it goes
C0017.4V1/3451 (Pro) or 7 (Standard)Leader joint 2; in Standard kits also all 6 follower joints
C0447.4V1/1912Leader joints 1 and 3
C0467.4V1/1473Leader joints 4, 5 and 6
C018 or C04712V1/3456 (Pro only)All 6 follower joints

Take your marker and write on every motor: L or F (leader or follower) and its joint number 1–6. For example the two C044 motors become L1 and L3. In a Standard kit the seven C001 motors are interchangeable: pick any one as L2 and the other six are F1–F6.

Why bother labelling?

The most common assembly mistake reported by builders is putting a leader motor with the wrong gear ratio into a joint. You only notice after the arm is fully built, and fixing it means taking the arm apart. Thirty seconds with a marker now saves an hour later. Chapter 2 explained why the leader's gears differ.

Step 4: check everything else is there

Kits differ between vendors, so your list may not match exactly. If a part seems to be missing, check the vendor's product page for the parts list of your exact version.

Safety rules for the whole course

The arm is small, but it is a machine that can move suddenly. These rules come from the vendor's safety notes.

  • Clamp the arm to the table before any program moves it. An unclamped arm will tip itself over.
  • Switch the power off before plugging or unplugging any motor cable, USB cable or power connector.
  • Keep the area clear. Remove drinks, phones and anything fragile from within the arm's reach, and keep faces and fingers out of the way while it is powered.
  • If anything looks or sounds wrong (shaking, grinding, a cable pulled tight), stop the program with Ctrl+C and unplug the power before you investigate.
  • Let the motors rest. A motor that strains against something it cannot move gets hot. If one feels hot to the touch, power off for a few minutes.

3D printing the parts (motors-only kits)

Open this if your kit has no plastic parts

The designs are free in the SO-ARM100 repository on GitHub, in the folder STL/SO101. If your school has a 3D printer, these are the tested settings from the designers:

SettingValue
MaterialPLA+
Nozzle and layer height0.4 mm nozzle at 0.2 mm layers (or 0.6 mm nozzle at 0.4 mm layers)
Infill15%
SupportsEverywhere, but ignore slopes steeper than 45°; no supports inside horizontal screw holes

All parts for one arm are packed into a single file that fits the print bed: use Ender_Follower_SO101.stl and Ender_Leader_SO101.stl for a 220 × 220 mm bed, or the Prusa_… files for a 205 × 250 mm bed.

Print a test gauge first. The STL/Gauges folder has small test pieces. Print one and check that a servo fits snugly. If it is too tight or too loose, your printer needs adjusting before you spend many hours printing the full set.

Community builders report roughly 8–16 hours of printing for both arms. After printing, remove all support material; a small screwdriver slid underneath it works well.

No printer? The repository lists printing services, and several vendors sell the printed parts on their own. Note that vendor-printed parts can differ slightly from the official files, so buy the parts from the same vendor as your control boards if you can.
